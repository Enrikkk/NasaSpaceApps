## Q1. Agricultural zones of the Región de Murcia: what is grown where, areas and production

### Takeaway
In 2024 Murcia had about 360,000 ha of cropland: 194,630 ha rainfed (secano) and 165,344 ha irrigated (regadío). The irrigated vegetable belt is wider than the Campo de Cartagena. By CREM's 2024 municipal figures, Lorca/Águilas (Valle del Guadalentín and the southern coast) now have more lettuce and far more broccoli than Cartagena/Torre-Pacheco. Melon is still concentrated in the Campo de Cartagena. Rainfed almond (77,079 ha), vineyard (Jumilla and Yecla) and cereals (Caravaca, Moratalla, Lorca, Mula) dominate the interior.

## Q2. Typical current rotations, cropping calendars, irrigation needs and known problems

### Takeaway
Irrigated vegetable land in SE Spain typically carries 2 (sometimes 3) short cycles per year. A winter leafy or brassica crop is grown, and a spring–summer cucurbit or another leafy/brassica crop follows. Law caps this at 2 cycles per plot per year, or 3 only for leafy crops under 45 days. Water use is roughly 4,500 m³/ha (leafy) to 7,000 m³/ha (fruiting vegetables) net per crop, according to older Segura basin planning figures. Known problems are nitrate leaching to the Mar Menor and its aquifer, nematodes and soil-borne disease in repeated vegetables, salinity of irrigation water and erosion on rainfed slopes. Rainfed systems are cereal–fallow or almond monoculture with very low yields in drought years.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal cap on cycles (both Mar Menor zones):**
  - Art. 39.1 promotes crop rotation.
  - Art. 39.2 limits each plot to two annual cycles. Leafy vegetables with cycles under 45 days may have up to three. Sowing and harvest dates must be logged.
  - Source: [Ley 3/2020, consolidated text, BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es-mc/l/2020/07/27/3/con). Read via WebFetch; verify wording.
- **Zona 1 crop groups:**
  - Art. 51.1 splits crops into two groups by root depth and management. Grupo 1 is brassicas, leafy vegetables, onion, leek and aromatic herbs. Grupo 2 is peas, beans, melon, cucumber, pepper, tomato and potato.
  - Art. 51.2: at most 2 cycles per year, and only one may be Grupo 1.
  - Art. 51.3: two consecutive Grupo 1 cycles are banned.
  - Art. 51.6: irrigated land left bare for more than 2 months in autumn–winter needs a grass-type cover crop.
  - Source: [Ley 3/2020, BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es-mc/l/2020/07/27/3/con). The table in the source is partly garbled, so verify group placements.
- **Common outdoor crops in the Campo de Cartagena** are lettuce, broccoli, artichoke, melon, watermelon and cauliflower (MAPA 2012). Iceberg is the traditional lettuce type there. — [Vida Rural 2012](https://www.mapa.gob.es/ministerio/pags/Biblioteca/Revistas/pdf_Vrural/Vrural_2012_344_15_18.pdf)
- **Net irrigation doses by crop group, Zona Litoral / Campo de Cartagena units 57–59.** These come from the old Plan Hidrológico del Segura, Anejo 3 "Demanda agraria". The cycle is not dated in the document; it references the draft Plan Nacional de Regadíos, so probably late 1990s. ### Inferences
- Typical current sequences, as unsourced expert synthesis to verify with OCA Cartagena-Mar Menor or a cooperative agronomist:
  - lettuce (Sep/Oct–Dec/Jan) → lettuce or broccoli (Jan–Apr) → melon (Mar/Apr–Jul/Aug);
  - broccoli (Aug/Sep–Nov/Dec) → lettuce (Dec–Mar) → melon or fallow;
  - artichoke as a semi-perennial (Jul/Aug planting, harvest Nov–May).
  - Under Art. 51 the first two sequences are no longer legal in Zona 1 if both cycles are Grupo 1 (lettuce/brassica). The law effectively forces "Grupo 1 + Grupo 2" pairs, e.g., lettuce + melon, or broccoli + pea/bean.
- Month-by-month scoring calendar for the engine, using the FAO-56 Mediterranean lengths above:
  - Winter leafy/brassica crops occupy Sep–Apr. Spring–summer cucurbits occupy Mar–Sep. The two crops overlap only if the plot is double-cropped tightly.
  - A legume or cover window is Oct–Feb (faba/vetch–oat) or a summer catch crop Jun–Sep (sorghum/sudan grass, cowpea) after melon.
- Water budget per ha per year for scoring:
  - Leafy (4,500) + fruiting (7,000) gives about 11,500 m³/ha/yr net for a lettuce + melon double crop.
  - Leafy + legume (3,100) gives about 7,600.
  - These use the old PHCS crop-group doses above. Replace with FAO-56 ETc × local ETo where possible.

## Q3. Regulation: Ley 3/2020 Mar Menor, nitrate programme, CAP GAEC 6/7 and eco-schemes (as of Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
In the Mar Menor basin, Ley 3/2020 already reads like a rotation rulebook:
- at most 2 cycles per year (leafy <45 d excepted);
- in Zona 1, only one Grupo 1 (brassica/leafy) cycle and never two consecutive;
- no bare irrigated soil for more than 2 months in autumn–winter (cover crop required);
- 5% of each farm in nutrient-retention features;
- contour cultivation;
- a 1,500 m fertiliser-restriction band;
- a ban on ureic N and on N in base dressing.

Region-wide, a new nitrate action programme (general + specific Campo de Cartagena) was still in process in late 2025. The 2016 Order remained in force. CAP GAEC 7 requires 33% annual rotation plus every parcel rotated at least once in 3 years, or a diversification alternative. Eco-scheme P3 pays for rotation with ≥10% "improving" species (legumes reducible to 2.5% in arid zones under 400 mm).

### Cited Findings
**Ley 3/2020, de 27 de julio, de recuperación y protección del Mar Menor.** Source for all items: [consolidated text, BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es-mc/l/2020/07/27/3/con); article contents read via WebFetch, verify exact wording.
- Consolidated text last updated 06/09/2023. Earlier modifications are dated 22/06/2021, 30/08/2021, 22/09/2021, 01/06/2022 and 03/08/2023.
- Zones: Zona 1 and Zona 2, delimited in Anexo I (Art. 2.2). The former Zona 3 was merged into Zona 2. Farms in both zones apply each zone's rules to the part in it (Art. 26).
- **Allowed farming types.** Art. 27.1 promotes a shift to dryland crops, confined cultivation with nutrient recirculation, and sustainable and precision agriculture. Art. 50.1 allows only sustainable and precision agriculture in Zona 1.
- **Cycles and rotation.** Arts. 39 and 51 (see Q2) set:
  - at most 2 cycles per year;
  - in Zona 1, only one Grupo 1 cycle and no two consecutive Grupo 1 cycles;
  - a cover crop if irrigated soil stays bare for more than 2 months in autumn–winter (not greenhouses).
- **Erosion and runoff.**
  - Cultivation must follow contour lines (Art. 38.1).
  - Irrigated farms must install vegetated conservation structures (Art. 36.1–2; Anexo III).
  - Dryland farms need perimeter vegetated strips: 1 m wide on slopes <2%, 2 m on steeper slopes, untilled (Art. 36.4).
  - 5% of each farm in Zona 1 and 2 must be nutrient-retention systems: vegetated strips, filter strips, wetlands/ponds or cover crops (Art. 37.1–2).
  - Dryland farms with terraces are exempt from the retention-area requirement (Art. 37.6).
  - Terraces are a possible additional Zona 1 measure (Art. 54h).
  - Greenhouses must store at least 100 L/m² of runoff (Art. 41.2).
- **1,500 m band** from the Mar Menor shore (Art. 29):
  - fertilisers, manures and green manure banned, except organic/precision crops more than 500 m away;
  - fertilisation above 170 kg N/ha/yr banned;
  - 20% of each parcel reserved for listed uses;
  - no new or expanded greenhouses.
- **Fertilisation rules (Art. 40):**
  - technical prescription and logging required;
  - urea and ureic fertilisers banned;
  - ammonium nitrate only under technical supervision, and banned in the last third of vegetable cycles;
  - no mineral N in base dressing;
  - N balance mandatory, using the lowest extraction coefficient in the action programme;
  - soil nitrate >100 mg/kg requires an extra depletion factor;
  - P fertilisers banned when P-Olsen exceeds 120 mg/kg.
- **Zona 1 extras:**
  - only composted manures (Art. 52);
  - soil-moisture sensors or tensiometers on irrigated farms over 0.5 ha (Art. 53.1);
  - drippers above 2.2 L/h banned on vegetables (Art. 53.2).

- **GAEC/BCAM 7 (crop rotation), from the 2025 single application.** Source: [FEGA BCAM 7 sheet, reproduced by AgroNews CyL](https://www.agronewscastillayleon.com/ficha-fega-bcam7/)

### Inferences
- For a Campo de Cartagena field, any recommended strategy must be filtered by hard legal constraints before scoring:
  - at most 2 cycles per year;

## Q4. Water: Tajo-Segura transfer, desalination, allocations, aquifers and prices

### Takeaway
The 2024/25 hydrological year was moderately good: 489 hm³ of Tajo-Segura transfers were authorised. The Segura system nonetheless entered "alerta" (scarcity) in Nov–Dec 2025. Higher Tajo ecological flows (upheld by the Supreme Court, applied from 2026) are expected to cut transfers by tens of hm³ per year. In Oct 2026 the aqueduct was under emergency repair. Desalination already supplies about 260 hm³/yr in the basin, at subsidised irrigation prices of about 0.33–0.38 €/m³ versus 0.60–0.80 €/m³ production cost. The CHS says farmers are near their "capacity to pay" limit. Aquifers are overexploited; Alto Guadalentín subsidence reaches about 10 cm/yr.

## Q5. Climate stressors: warming, heat, torrential rain/DANA, erosion, frost, drought

### Takeaway
Murcia is warming fast:
- more than 2 °C in about 70 years (OSERM);
- Jan–Oct 2026 tied 2024 as the warmest on record (regional mean 19.2 °C);
- tropical nights are routine (31 of 31 nights in July 2026 at Murcia Observatory).

Torrential "DANA" rains hit the Campo de Cartagena repeatedly: Sept 2019, Oct 2024, "Alice" Oct 2025, and Oct 2026 with a record 180 l/m² in one day at San Javier. These events wash nitrate and sediment into the Mar Menor. The Segura basin was in prolonged drought Feb 2023–Jul 2024, and rainfed almond lost more than 30% in 2025. Spring frost and hail remain episodic risks for almond in the Altiplano and Noroeste.

## Q6. Agronomy of rotations suited to semi-arid Mediterranean irrigated and rainfed systems (quantitative where possible)

### Takeaway
Local (Murcia/SE Spain) evidence supports four kinds of change:
1. Intercropping or rotating legumes with brassicas and melon. In Diverfarming (Cartagena) this gave a 30% fertiliser reduction with stable or higher yields.
2. Green manure or reduced tillage in rainfed almond and cereals. Erosion fell 65–85% and SOC rose 30–45%.
3. Brassica biofumigation/biosolarization against soil pathogens. It works well against fungi but nematode control is irregular.
4. Salt- and drought-tolerant crops (barley, sorghum/sudan grass, durum wheat, cowpea, safflower, pistachio) as alternatives under water scarcity or salinity.

Legume N fixation in Mediterranean conditions is roughly 60–120 kg N/ha.

### Cited Findings
- **Legume N fixation:**
  - Typical values: vetch 100–120, pea about 100, chickpea 60–80 and common bean about 50 kg N/ha/yr. — [CSIC, Arbor article (table on biological N fixation)](https://arbor.revistas.csic.es/index.php/arbor/article/download/2115/2769/4289). Column alignment in the extracted PDF was unclear; verify.
  - Faba bean fixes about 90 kg N/ha in shoots, about 130 kg N/ha including roots. This is a European, not specifically Mediterranean, figure. — [Pommeresche & Hansen 2017, Organic e-prints](https://orgprints.dk/id/eprint/31344/7/pommeresche-hansen-2017-root-nodules-spanish.pdf)
  - Fixation drops under water stress, so it is lower in dry, unirrigated summers. — [CSIC Arbor](https://arbor.revistas.csic.es/index.php/arbor/article/download/2115/2769/4289)
- **Diverfarming (H2020), Campo de Cartagena:**
  - Melon intercropped with cowpea raised melon yield by 34–70% with 30% less fertiliser.
  - In the first year a CEBAS-CSIC/UPCT/UMU study measured significant increases in total N, available P and total organic C in the soil.
  - Source: [Interempresas, Diverfarming results](https://www.interempresas.net/Horticola/376565-El-proyecto-Diverfarming-presenta-sus-resultados-de-diversificacion-de-cultivos.html)
  - Broccoli intercropped with faba bean at the AsociaHortus farm (Cartagena) cut fertiliser use by 30% with stable broccoli yield and higher soil moisture. — [eComercioAgrario](https://ecomercioagrario.com/?p=56894)
- **Rainfed almond diversification (CEBAS-CSIC):**
  - Adding perennials (e.g., thyme) between rainfed almonds reduced carbon losses by 65–73% over two years.
  - Annual crops between irrigated mandarins did not improve the carbon balance (Plant and Soil, 2021).
  - Source: [AgroNews CyL](https://www.agronewscastillayleon.com/la-introduccion-de-plantas-perennes-entre-almendros-en-secano-ayuda-mitigar-el-cambio-climatico)
- **Reduced tillage and green manure in rainfed Murcia:**
  - Moving to reduced tillage plus residues raised SOC stocks by 30% at Alhagüeces. At Burete, green manure raised SOC by 45% over reduced tillage alone. — [FAO-hosted summary (Martínez-Mena/Almagro, CEBAS-CSIC)](https://www.fao.org/3/a-bs088e.pdf)
  - Erosion fell by about 65% (wheat) and 85% (almond). — [CATENA 2020 summary](https://www.thepoultrysite.com/news/2020/11/sustainable-farming-practices-could-save-mediterranean-soils)
  - Caveat: a 2013 paper found lower foliar N and almond yield during the transition to organic/reduced tillage. — [Martínez-Mena et al. 2013, Mitig. Adapt. Strateg. Glob. Change](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11027-013-9535-2)
- **LIFE AMDRYC4 (UMU-led, ended 2022):** this project studied climate adaptation of dryland systems (cover crops, carbon) on four Murcia farms: El Moralejo (Caravaca), Xiquena and Nogalte (Lorca), Corvera (Murcia). I found no quantitative published outputs. — [LIFE AMDRYC4](https://lifeamdryc4.eu/en/life-project/); [AdapteCCa case study](https://adaptecca.es/en/casos-practicos/life-amdryc4-project-adaptation-climate-change-rainfed-agricultural-systems)
- **Biofumigation/biosolarization:**
  - Growing brassicas plus biosolarization reduced *M. incognita* populations and early root damage in pepper, improving yield. — [ITEA 2016](https://recyt.fecyt.es/index.php/ITEA/article/view/38681)
  - A seven-year biosolarization trial in a Campo de Cartagena clay-loam was benchmarked against methyl bromide. — [MAPA, Spanish horticulture journal 2006](https://www.mapa.gob.es/ministerio/pags/Biblioteca/Revistas/pdf%5FSH%2FSH%5F2006%5F14%5F21%5F30%2Epdf)
  - Biosolarization combined with resistant rootstocks is "an effective and stable" approach against *Meloidogyne* (Ros et al. 2007). — [Vida Rural 2007](https://mapa.gob.es/ministerio/pags/Biblioteca/Revistas/pdf%5FVrural%2FVrural%5F2007%5F244%5F72%5F74%2Epdf)
- **Salt tolerance of alternatives.** ECe threshold → ECe at 50% yield, dS/m:
  - barley 8.0 → 18; sorghum 6.8 → 9.9; wheat 6.0 → 13; durum 5.7 → 15;
  - cowpea 4.9 → 9.1; common vetch 3.0 → 7.6; Sudan grass 2.8 → 14; alfalfa 2.0 → 8.8; broad bean 1.5 → 6.8.
  - Safflower, oats, olive, fig and pomegranate are "moderately tolerant". Sunflower, melon and watermelon are "moderately sensitive".
  - Source: [FAO-29 Table 4 and qualitative list](https://www.fao.org/4/t0234e/T0234E03.htm)
- **Pistachio as a dryland alternative:** in Castilla-La Mancha 68,311 ha were planted by 2025, 17,848 ha of the productive area rainfed. — [AgroNews CyL](https://www.agronewscastillayleon.com/cosecha-pistacho-castilla-la-mancha-2026-8054-toneladas) (via search summary). Root depth 1.0–1.5 m and Kc mid 1.10 (FAO-56) mean it is not a low-water crop if irrigated.

### Inferences
Candidate strategy set for the scoring engine. Each is legal in Zona 1/2 and backed by local evidence.

**Irrigated Campo de Cartagena / Guadalentín:**
- **S0, current:** lettuce (winter) → melon (summer), or lettuce → broccoli. The latter is now illegal as consecutive Grupo 1 in Zona 1.
- **S1, legume swap:** lettuce/broccoli (winter) → faba bean or pea (spring, Grupo 2). Fixes about 90–130 kg N/ha, uses about 3,100 m³/ha versus 7,000 for melon, and qualifies for eco-scheme P3. Risk: lower gross margin.
- **S2, diversified cash:** broccoli + faba intercrop (winter) → melon + cowpea intercrop (summer). Diverfarming shows −30% fertiliser and +34–70% melon yield. Risk: management complexity, market for faba/cowpea.
- **S3, soil-health break:** melon → summer sorghum-sudangrass or brassica cover (mustard) incorporated with biosolarization → winter lettuce. Targets nematodes/fungi and nitrate catch; Sudan grass and sorghum are salt-tolerant.

**Rainfed interior (Noroeste/Altiplano/Mula):**
- **S0:** cereal–fallow (barbecho), or almond with tilled bare soil.
- **S1:** barley–vetch (or vetch-oat forage)–fallow. Vetch is about 100–120 kg N/ha, and forage helps the local sheep sector.
- **S2:** barley–chickpea or lentil. Lentil and chickpea fetch good prices (MAPA 2026: lentils about 1,112 €/t, chickpea about 561 €/t versus barley about 204 €/t).
- **S3, for almond:** spontaneous or sown cover in alternate rows plus reduced tillage. Erosion −85%, SOC +30–45%; eco-scheme P6 pays 59–131 €/ha depending on slope. Risk: water competition in dry springs.

### Gaps
- I found no Murcia-specific measured N-fixation data (kg N/ha) for faba, vetch or chickpea under drip irrigation.
- I found no published nitrate-leaching reduction (%) from winter cover crops in the Campo de Cartagena.
- I found no quantitative LIFE AMDRYC4 or LIFE Agrimed results; LIFE Sinergias was not found.
- I found no local yield or price data for quinoa, caper or safflower in Murcia.
- The ¹⁵N chickpea-vs-faba comparison in Andalusia (López-Bellido) was mentioned in search results but not read.
