Captured via Chrome on 2026-11-10. Anything not published yet is marked [NOT LOADED]. Quotes are exact where in quotation marks.

IMPORTANT TERMINOLOGY: NASA's 2026 pages use "Super Intelligence (SI)" instead of "AI". Per the FAQ: "In accordance with the September 29, 2026 U.S. Executive Order, 'the executive branch shall use the terms "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI."'" So "SI-powered" in a challenge title means AI-powered.

## 2. What a submission must contain
Sources: FAQ (https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/participant-faqs/); Terms (https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/); structure of 2025 winning project pages (e.g. https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/pureflow2/?tab=project).

- Submitted via the Project tab of your team page on spaceappschallenge.org ("Edit Project"). Required "to receive a participant certificate and to be eligible for Global Judging".
- Project Submission Guide (exact field list, video length, slide limits, repo/link rules): [NOT LOADED] - due November 13. No numeric limits for demo video length, slides or file sizes could be verified.
- Observed project-page sections on 2025 winners (likely same in 2026, UNVERIFIED for 2026): Summary; Project Demonstration (a video link: YouTube or Google Drive/Canva link were used); Project (a link to the live app or a Drive folder); Project Details (long free text); Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI); NASA Data (list); Space Agency Partner & Other Data (list). The chosen Challenge text is shown automatically.
- SI/AI disclosure is mandatory in 2026: "Each team's project submission must include a description of how SI tools were used during the development of their project." (FAQ).
- Terms: "You must cite every source that is used in the creation of your project, even if it is open source or freely available." You are "prompted to submit references on the Project Submission form". "NASA also reserves the right to remove nominations, awards, or winner status from teams that do not cite resources used in their Solution; all resources, even open source resources, must be cited."
- Solutions must "directly address one or more of the NASA Space Apps Challenge statements". To be eligible for Global Judging and Global Awards "teams must respond to one of the challenges".
- Licensing: original content must be "freely available without restriction or ... licensed as open source as defined by the Open Source Initiative". If no OSI license is specified, "NASA will distribute your original content under an Apache 2.0 license." Non-NASA materials (datasets/code not from NASA or space agency partners) must be freely and publicly available or open-source licensed, and their licenses "must be identified in the documentation". NASA will not accept or post Solutions lacking license documentation for third-party materials. You are responsible for ensuring no export-controlled (ITAR/EAR) information.
- Not accepted/posted: threatening/obscene/discriminatory content; sexually explicit material; "Material that promotes commercial services or products"; proprietary or business-sensitive information; things you do not hold IP rights to; personally identifiable information. "Recognizable names, voices, and likenesses of persons under the age of 18 should not be included in any submission" (and no under-18 likenesses in videos).
- Videos: must be your original work; no third-party infringement; written consent of any individual shown; grants the U.S. government a royalty-free, worldwide, perpetual, non-exclusive license.

## 3. Team rules
Sources: FAQ; Team Formation Guide (https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/team-formation-guide/); home page https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/

- Maximum team size: six (6) members ("Teams may have no more than six (6) members"). Recommended 4-5.
- Minimum for global judging: Local Event teams at least 1 person; Universal Event teams at least 2 people.
- You may be on only one team; each team works on only one challenge (Team Owner can change the challenge via "Edit Details").
- All team members must be registered for the same Local Event (or the Universal Event). The Team Owner's Local Event represents the team. "All team members must be confirmed participants at the same Local Event in order for your team's project to be eligible for awards." Waitlisted members must be accepted by the Local Lead or switch event.
- Team formation opened Sep 17; members can be added until the hackathon ends Nov 15, 11:59 pm local time. One person is the Team Owner (approves requests, must post team contact info).
- Participants are not expected to submit fully developed projects: "participants are not expected to submit a fully developed project at the end" (two-day event).
- Under 18: must be registered by a parent/guardian and accompanied at all times (Local Events may add requirements); no photos/likeness of minors in avatar, team page, project demo or submission.
- Eligibility/other (Terms, updated Aug 7, 2026): US federal civil servants are not eligible; NASA policy bars participation or collaboration "in connection with an affiliation with China or a Chinese-owned entity" (based on affiliation, not nationality). No cost to participate - report any request to pay to info@spaceappschallenge.org. Conduct concerns: conduct@spaceappschallenge.org.

## 4. Judging process and award categories
Sources: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/awards/ ; FAQ ; Team Formation Guide

- Stages: (1) Local Event / Universal Event judges choose Global Nominees (first round; "your team's project will be evaluated by the Local Judges at the Local Event for which your team is registered"). (2) NASA and Space Agency Partner subject-matter experts review all Global Nominees to pick Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions. (3) An executive committee of NASA and Space Agency Partner agency leaders picks the 10 Global Winners from the Global Finalists.
- Universal Event: judged in the same overall period but "evaluated only in comparison with other Universal Event submissions"; selection rate for Global Nominees said to be comparable.
- Participation certificate: every participant who submits a project gets a certificate on their profile page.
- 10 Global Awards and their definitions:
  - Best Use of Science: "The project that makes the best and most valid use of science and/or the scientific method."
  - Best Use of Data: "The project that best makes space data accessible or leverages it for a unique application."
  - Best Use of Technology: "The project that exemplifies the most innovative use of technology."
  - Galactic Impact: "The project with the most potential to improve life on Earth or in the universe."
  - Best Mission Concept: "The project with the most plausible concept and design."
  - Most Inspirational: "The project that captures our hearts."
  - Best Use of Storytelling: "The project that most creatively communicates the potential of open data through the art of storytelling."
  - Global Connection: "The project that best connects people around the world through technology."
  - Art & Technology: "The project that most effectively combines technical and creative skills."
  - Local Impact: "The project that demonstrates the greatest potential for local impact."

- 2026 timing: nominees/finalists/honorable mentions announced December 2026, winners January 2027 (in 2025, winners were announced December 18, 2025 per the 2025 awards page).

## 5. Rules on AI / SI (generative AI) use
Source: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/participant-faqs/ ("What is Super Intelligence? Can teams utilize it during the hackathon?")

- Allowed: "Participants may use SI tools to accelerate innovation, streamline development, and enhance creativity in support of their challenge solution."
- Definition of SI: "computer systems that perform tasks typically associated with human reasoning, decision-making, content creation, and problem-solving. Examples include large language models, conversational assistants, image and video generation tools, foundation models, and code generation tools."
- Required: "Each team's project submission must include a description of how SI tools were used during the development of their project." (2025 winners filled a "Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI)" section, listing tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Lovable, Bolt, ElevenLabs, and stating what the team did vs what AI did.)
- Note: several 2026 challenges are themselves about building SI-powered tools (e.g. Flame in Freefall); no extra restrictions are stated on the challenge pages.
- Additional constraints that touch generative content: third-party materials need open licenses; videos must not infringe copyright; no likeness of under-18s; cite all sources.

## 7. Eligibility for Global Awards (summary)
Sources: home page; FAQ; Team Formation Guide; Terms
- Be registered for the 2026 Space Apps Challenge (account + registration form + Terms signed) and chosen to a Local Event or the Universal Event.
- Submit a project via the team's Project tab before the deadline (Nov 15, 11:59 PM local) - needed for certificate and Global Judging.
- Team of at most 6; all members registered at the same Local Event; Universal Event teams at least 2 people.
- Respond to one of the challenges.
- Cite all sources; disclose SI use; respect licensing/IP/minor-likeness rules.
- Participation by US federal civil servants and China-affiliated entities is not permitted (Terms).

## 8. Space Apps Connect
Sources: FAQ; https://github.com/nasa/spaceapps (linked from the site banner "Space Apps Connect is now open!"); Resources page lists a Space Apps Connect Guide (not at the guessed URL /resources/space-apps-connect-guide/, which returned 404 - guide titles: "Introduction to Space Apps Connect - 2026", "How to Search Space Apps Connect", "How to Post on Space Apps Connect")
- "the collaborative communication forum for the NASA Space Apps Challenge community." Free for registered participants, optional but "highly encouraged". Opens November 3 and closes at the end of the hackathon.
- Lets participants talk with other participants, Local Leads, Navigators, SMEs, Global Collaborators and the Global Organizing (GO) Team. Has a "Find a Team" category.
- Questions about challenges or datasets "can be asked in Space Apps Connect in the week prior to and during the hackathon. NASA Space Apps Navigators and NASA subject matter experts will be available to answer your challenge and data-related questions."
